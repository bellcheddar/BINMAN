This is a geometric filter, not a classifier, and it has now been measured. Against the matched zinc-finger screen of Sievers et al. 2018 (32 domains depleted by an IMiD against 5,476 assayed in the same screens and not depleted) it reaches sensitivity 0.66 and specificity 0.35, with an ROC AUC of 0.44. That is below chance. The column is named degron_geometry_score because it is a rank over hairpin geometry, not a probability of degradation; a C2H2 zinc finger is a short hairpin with an exposed glycine, so this score largely detects the fold. Use it to generate hypotheses, not to triage. Full contingency table and threshold sweep on the About tab.
Two scores, two questions. degron_geometry_score ranks hairpin geometry and is the one that scores below chance above. imid_degradation_score is a different question: the predicted probability that a C2H2 zinc finger is degraded by pomalidomide, from a sequence model validated on the Sievers degrome with features weighted by the Slabicki alanine scan. It reaches a nested held-out AUC of 0.830 at sensitivity 0.867 and specificity 0.613, which clears the spec 9.2 floors that the geometry misses.

It is empty for most rows on purpose: 4,650 of 21,717 candidates carry a zinc finger, and the model has nothing to say about the rest. It is also specific to pomalidomide. The same features predict lenalidomide well and the promiscuous analogs barely at all, which is a finding rather than a limitation: degradability is compound-specific.
